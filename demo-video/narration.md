# DeliveryLens demo film: narration script

About 6 minutes, around 880 words, read at a calm launch pace (about 145 words a minute).
Storytelling rules used:
- **ABC:** accuracy (every number comes from the repo's docs), brevity, clarity.
- **One hook:** "Imagine…".
- **Primacy:** the main message comes in the first minute.
- **Recency:** the same message closes the film.
- **Frequency:** "DeliveryLens" and its promise, *find why, fix it, prove it*, repeat in every chapter.

Each line is one caption on screen. `[ ]` = what the picture shows.

---

## 0. Hook (0:00–0:35)
[Dawn. Smoky warehouse, a loading dock, one empty space where a truck should be.]
1. Imagine you run a busy shop.
2. Your stock was promised for this morning. You told your own customers it would be on the shelf by noon.
3. Noon comes. The truck does not.
4. Nobody calls. Nobody explains.
[Text slams in: 29 of every 100]
5. At Halcyon Logistics, this happens to twenty-nine of every hundred deliveries.

## 1. The problem (0:35–1:20)
[US map at night, 48,340 delivery lines lighting up; some burn orange.]
6. Halcyon delivers across the United States. In six months: forty-eight thousand, three hundred and forty deliveries.
7. Only seventy-one in a hundred arrived on time. The industry benchmark is ninety-four.
8. Late deliveries cost about one point seven four million dollars a year in penalties.
9. The customer success team spends sixty-four percent of its week answering complaints. And in twelve of those complaints, the client says the same thing: "nobody told us."
10. Nine big clients, worth fourteen point two million dollars a year, are thinking of leaving.
[A price tag swings in: $680K]
11. The plan on the table: buy new routing software, for six hundred and eighty thousand dollars.
12. But is the route really the problem?

## 2. Primacy: what DeliveryLens is (1:20–1:40)
[Smoke clears. Logo draws its route line.]
13. This is DeliveryLens.
14. DeliveryLens finds why deliveries are late. It fixes each cause, every day. And it proves the gain, with Halcyon's own data.
15. Find why. Fix it. Prove it.

## 3. Find why (1:40–2:35)
[Real app: Why deliveries are late, the bars grow.]
16. DeliveryLens read every one of the forty-eight thousand deliveries.
17. Five causes explain twenty-eight of the twenty-nine points lost.
18. A weak carrier on certain lanes. An incomplete address. A same-day order that arrives after the eight o'clock pick list. Stock that is not at the nearest warehouse. And a driver who leaves the planned route.
[Real app: How it works, the two US maps, today and with DeliveryLens]
19. Here is the same order, played twice. Today, the nearest warehouse is empty, so the truck crosses the country and arrives late. With DeliveryLens, a warehouse that has the stock ships it, and it arrives on time.
[Real app: Vendor decision and What-if simulator]
20. And here is the surprise. The routing tool alone lifts on-time delivery to about eighty. Fixing the five causes lifts it to about ninety.
21. You can test any mix of fixes yourself, in the what-if simulator.

## 4. Fix it, every day (2:35–4:40)
[Smoky transition into a warehouse office at 07:00. Email icons arrive.]
22. Now, the daily work. Orders arrive by email, in fourteen different layouts. Staff spend forty-four hours a week typing them in.
[Real app: Order desk, an email selected, the envelope travels the ten steps]
23. On the DeliveryLens Order desk, an email goes in, and a checked warehouse order comes out.
24. A safety screen checks the email first. Then the AI reads it: the client, the items, the address, the deadline.
25. And then the rules decide. Is the address complete? Is the stock at the nearest warehouse? Which carrier is strongest on this lane? Can we keep the promise?
26. The AI reads. The rules decide. And a person approves, before anything reaches the warehouse system.
[Real app: the missing-postcode email; the envelope stops, orange]
27. What if the postcode is missing? DeliveryLens finds the one past delivery site that matches, and asks the client to confirm it with one click.
[Real app: the client's confirm page]
28. The client sees one address and one button. No login. Confirmed, and the order moves on.
[Real app: the 08:00 clock]
29. An order that arrives at five past eight misses the truck and waits a day. DeliveryLens adds a grace hour, and an urgent window until ten. That alone avoids about seven hundred and twenty-nine late deliveries a year.
[Real app: Morning risk board]
30. At eight every morning, the risk board ranks today's shipments by how likely they are to be late, and shows the fix that is still possible.
31. We tested it on two months it had never seen. Of its daily top twenty, sixty-three percent really were late, against twenty-nine percent overall.
[Real app: Stock and routes cards]
32. Stock and routes shows, in three pictures, why some orders take the long way.

## 5. Protect the money and the clients (4:40–5:25)
[Real app: Carriers, penalty recovery]
33. DeliveryLens also finds the penalties Halcyon paid where the carrier alone was at fault: two hundred and sixty-one thousand, nine hundred dollars in six months. The AI drafts the claim letter.
[Real app: At-risk clients, a save plan]
34. For each of the nine at-risk clients, DeliveryLens writes a save plan: talking points, an agenda, and an email, from that client's own deliveries.
[Real app: Weekly brief, Ask DeliveryLens, Agent activity]
35. Every Monday, the leaders get a one-page brief. Anyone can ask a question in plain English. And every AI call is logged, with its cost and its guardrail checks.

## 6. Bad input, handled (5:25–5:50)
[Real app: the injection email, the safety screen turns red]
36. What about bad input? This email hides an instruction: "ignore all previous instructions."
37. DeliveryLens blocks it at the safety screen. Nothing reaches the warehouse.
[Real app: the vague order, Needs review]
38. And an unclear order is never guessed. It goes to a person.

## 7. Built to go live (5:50–6:10)
[Tests and AWS diagram]
39. DeliveryLens is live on AWS. Every push runs three hundred and eighty-seven backend tests and sixty frontend tests, then deploys itself.

## 8. Recency: the close (6:10–6:40)
[Smoke rises again. The dock from the hook, now a truck pulls in on time.]
40. So, back to your shop. The truck arrives. And if it is going to be late, you hear first.
[71 → 90 counts up]
41. From seventy-one to about ninety on time. About one point four million dollars a year. Without the six-hundred-and-eighty-thousand-dollar tool.
42. DeliveryLens. Find why. Fix it. Prove it.
[End card: DeliveryLens · Team InfinityStacks · EvoHack 2026]
