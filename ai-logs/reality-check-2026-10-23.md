## 10 hardest questions the "COO/CFO" judges are likely to ask

1. **"You say 96.6% if everything is fixed. Will I actually get 96.6%?"**
   No. That is the ceiling if every fix is fully adopted. With realistic adoption (address capture 70%, cutoff rule 80%, carrier rules 100%, stock 50%, route 50%), the simulator says about 86–88% in the first quarter. The rest depends on stock rebalancing and the route pilot. We will measure weekly against a control warehouse.

2. **"My vendor does carrier selection too. Your own chart gives routing 78.6%. Why not just sign?"**
   78.6% assumes the vendor fixes carrier choice and route deviation perfectly. Carrier choice is a rule change (stop sending D and F to their weak lanes) that costs about $42K a year in higher rates and needs no software. Route deviation, the part only software fixes, is worth 3 points. We would pilot the vendor on the high-deviation lanes before committing the $680K.

3. **"What does your plan cost, and when does it pay back?"**
   Answer from the ROI table (to be built). The anchors are a carrier uplift of about $42K a year, a second pick wave of X FTE-hours, rebalancing transfers of $Y, and AI run cost of about $Z per 1,000 emails. Against roughly $1.7M a year of SLA penalties, about half of that avoidable in the realistic case, payback is in months, not years. Do not answer this without real numbers.

4. **"The at-risk clients get 71% on time like everyone else. So why will fixing lateness keep them?"**
   Correct, lateness alone does not single them out. What does is the experience: 24 of the 34 complaints come from them, and 12 complaints say "nobody told us". That is why Delay Alert and the proactive notice are part of the plan. We propose agreeing a visible target with each of the nine accounts (e.g. 90% for 4 weeks).

5. **"Stopping same-day after 08:00 means losing half my same-day orders. Is that a fix?"**
   It is one option. The other is a second pick wave at about 11:00 that still meets the 14:00–18:00 collection window. 54% of same-day orders arrive after 08:00, so the right choice depends on the warehouse-capacity answer from Q3. We present both, with the trade-off.

6. **"How do you know these are causes and not just correlations?"**
   We cannot prove causation from history. What we have: the lane picks hold on unseen months (Apr–Jun picks, Jul–Sep test); the step at exactly 08:00 matches the process; and clean deliveries are 96–99% on time. Proof comes from the pilot design: a fix on one warehouse or lane, a control on another, and a 4-week read.

7. **"Carrier F is our preferred supplier. Are you telling me to break the contract?"**
   Not necessarily. F is 75% on time to the Midwest, as good as anyone. We keep F on the Midwest and move it off the Northeast and Southeast. Separately, Carrier D under-performs in every region once other causes are removed, so D volume is the first thing to move. Whether volume minimums allow this is our question to Procurement (Q5).

8. **"Every late delivery has exactly $34 of redelivery cost. Are your savings real?"**
   We flagged it ourselves as a probable recording rule. Our ROI is shown both with and without it. Penalties alone ($872K in 6 months) justify the plan.

9. **"Is your AI actually doing anything, or is it a demo?"**
   Show `agent_runs` with Bedrock model IDs, latency and tokens. Show the eval: field-level accuracy on N messy emails, the false-positive rate on address checks, and a blocked injection. KPIs are never computed by AI; AI reads emails and writes messages, and a human approves before anything is sent.

10. **"What happens when this goes to our real systems? What would make you change your recommendation?"**
    Next steps: a WMS/EDI integration for intake, carrier APIs for delay events (D, E and F have none), and a weekly attribution refresh. We would change our mind if the 4-week pilot of address check plus lane rules does not lift on-time by at least 5 points, or if the vendor can show route deviation is larger than the data says (for example, the 1,339 deliveries with no route record).
